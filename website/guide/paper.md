# Paper and ink

The paper is chosen independently of the theme, and it supplies the terminal's colours. Every paper keeps
ink above a contrast of 7:1, muted ink above 4.5:1 and every ANSI colour above 3:1; these are checked by the
project's tests.

| Paper | Character |
| --- | --- |
| Cotton | Cotton rag, felted rather than crumpled: a soft tooth of little rounded hills, as on cold-pressed watercolour paper, with faint threads in the pulp |
| Laid | Fine laid lines across the sheet, chain lines down it |
| Linen | A woven cross-hatch with slubs |
| Onionskin (default) | Thin and smooth, with only a gentle crinkle |
| Parchment | Mottled and aged, with foxing, tide lines and a fold |
| Night | A dark sheet for night work, with pale ink |

## Sheet width

*Sheet width* in the Paper & Ink section of the Studio sets how wide the sheet stands in the typewriter: from
the narrowest, with the platen's ends showing beside it, to the full length of the platen. The machine keeps its
size; the terminal gains the columns.

## Texture and impressions

*Texture depth* sets how deep the paper's relief is and how strongly its fibres catch the light. Each paper
keeps its own texture under the lamp and in the window's light; the light only makes it brighter.
*Type impression* sets how a character you type lands: for a moment it is heavier and its ink spreads a
little, then it settles into ordinary print. An impression is only ever drawn over a character the terminal
has already displayed, so hidden input such as a password never appears in it.

## Selecting text

Selected text lies under a soft, feathered wash instead of a flat box, and each character taken into the
selection dissolves like a drop of ink in water and re-forms. It follows the mouse while you drag, so you always see how far the selection reaches. Selecting and copying work exactly as before;
only the drawing changes. *Selection* in the Type section of the Studio switches between **Ink** and
**Classic**, xterm's own selection box.
